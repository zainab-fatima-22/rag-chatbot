# FBR Registration, Iris Filing and Active Taxpayer List — Individual Taxpayers

## Income-tax registration

FBR states that an individual is treated as registered when e-enrolled on the Iris portal. For an individual, the 13-digit CNIC is used as the NTN or registration number. Iris credentials provide access to the online income-tax system.

FBR's online registration requirements for an individual include a personal email address, a mobile/SIM registered against the individual's own CNIC, and specified supporting documents. Business owners may also need evidence relating to business premises and a recent paid utility bill.

## Filing through Iris

FBR states that income-tax returns are filed online through Iris. A first-time filer needs registration before filing. A person who already has an NTN or registration number but lacks Iris credentials can use the relevant e-enrollment option.

For completing an online income-tax return, FBR states that the taxpayer completes the Return of Income form and the Wealth Statement where applicable. A successful submission is confirmed when the relevant forms move from Draft to Completed Task in Iris.

For a salaried person who meets the conditions for the simplified salaried declaration, FBR provides Declaration form 114(I). FBR describes this as available to persons deriving income only from salary and other sources where salary is more than 50% of income.

## Record keeping

FBR states that persons having taxable income are required to keep income-tax return records for six years.

## Active Taxpayer List (ATL)

The Active Taxpayer List is FBR's central record of online income-tax return filers for the previous tax year. FBR also provides online and SMS methods for checking active taxpayer status.

For an individual, FBR's SMS check uses the format:

ATL [space] 13-digit CNIC

sent to 9966.

Being on the ATL can provide lower withholding or tax-deduction rates in a number of situations, subject to the specific statutory provision. FBR lists benefits including lower rates on certain bank deductions, motor-vehicle transactions, property transactions, securities capital gains, dividends, and prize-bond winnings, and the ability to claim back overpaid withheld tax where applicable.

## RAG instruction

For procedural questions, distinguish between registration, filing, and ATL status. Do not claim that registration automatically makes someone an ATL filer; ATL status is linked to filing and the applicable FBR rules.
