# Pakistan Salaried Individual Tax Slabs — Tax Year 2027

## Applicability

These slabs are from the First Schedule as amended by the Finance Act 2026 and apply from 1 July 2026 for the relevant salaried-individual tax table.

For this knowledge base, a salaried question should be interpreted using **Tax Year 2027** unless the user specifies another year.

## Annual taxable-income slabs

| Taxable income | Tax calculation |
|---|---|
| Up to Rs. 600,000 | 0% |
| Above Rs. 600,000 to Rs. 1,200,000 | Rs. 0 + 1% of amount exceeding Rs. 600,000 |
| Above Rs. 1,200,000 to Rs. 2,200,000 | Rs. 6,000 + 11% of amount exceeding Rs. 1,200,000 |
| Above Rs. 2,200,000 to Rs. 3,200,000 | Rs. 116,000 + 20% of amount exceeding Rs. 2,200,000 |
| Above Rs. 3,200,000 to Rs. 4,100,000 | Rs. 316,000 + 25% of amount exceeding Rs. 3,200,000 |
| Above Rs. 4,100,000 to Rs. 5,600,000 | Rs. 541,000 + 29% of amount exceeding Rs. 4,100,000 |
| Above Rs. 5,600,000 to Rs. 7,000,000 | Rs. 976,000 + 32% of amount exceeding Rs. 5,600,000 |
| Above Rs. 7,000,000 | Rs. 1,424,000 + 35% of amount exceeding Rs. 7,000,000 |

## How to use the slabs

The rates apply to **taxable income**, not automatically to gross salary. A user's taxable income can differ from gross salary because the Income Tax Ordinance and applicable rules determine what is included, exempt, deductible, or otherwise treated for tax purposes.

Example: if taxable income is Rs. 1,800,000, the slab formula is Rs. 6,000 + 11% of Rs. 600,000, giving Rs. 72,000 before considering any other applicable adjustments, credits, or withholding already paid.

Example: if taxable income is Rs. 5,000,000, the slab formula is Rs. 541,000 + 29% of Rs. 900,000, giving Rs. 802,000 before considering any other applicable adjustments, credits, or withholding already paid.

## High-income surcharge note

Do not automatically add an old 9% salaried surcharge rule to Tax Year 2027 calculations. The assistant should use the current Finance Act and current First Schedule applicable to the requested tax year rather than copying an older tax-year rule.
